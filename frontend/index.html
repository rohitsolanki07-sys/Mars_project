Mission orientation

FIRST CONTACT · OPTIONAL GUIDED TOUR

Welcome to Mars mission control

Want a guided, hands-on tour? We’ll walk through the map, route planner, science layers, mission readouts, and the rest of the console. You can try controls as you go, pause whenever you like, and replay the tour later with ?.

All routes and telemetry are for demonstration only. This application is not certified for real mission use.

01 / 15
SURFACE OPERATIONS · MAP CONTROLS

Move around the Mars globe

Map opens the full globe. Drag it to rotate, use + and − to zoom, and press ◎ to return to the global view. Try each control while this guide stays open.

Choose Full screen to resize the map. Move the pointer over the surface to read coordinates; select a mapped feature for its details. The sidebar arrow switches between Map and Planner.

02 / 15
MISSION SETUP · LANDING SITE

Choose a mission site

Use this selector to switch between Mount Sharp (Gale Crater) and Jezero Crater. The route, waypoints, and sample mission estimates update for the selected site. The ↻ button reloads the route.

Try switching sites, then return to your preferred one. Site data is a local demonstration dataset, not a live mission feed.

03 / 15
MISSION SETUP · SAVED WAYPOINTS

Set route start and destination

Choose a FROM and TO location from the menus. Options include saved mission waypoints and, when online data is available, named Mars features and science candidates.

Try changing an endpoint. The route summary updates; select a waypoint in the list to center the map on it, or use Overview to fit the route on screen.

04 / 15
MISSION SETUP · CUSTOM ENDPOINTS

Pick a point on the map or enter coordinates

Choose Pick start on map or Pick destination on map, then click the desired surface location. Open Enter custom coordinates to enter latitude and longitude directly instead.

Try either method; selecting a point on the map leaves the planner ready to continue. Coordinate inputs are in decimal degrees.

05 / 15
ROUTE CONTROL · TRAVEL MODES

Compare suit, rover, and scout estimates

Select Suit Walk, Rover, or Scout. The travel mode changes the modeled speed and resource estimates shown for the route.

Try each mode and compare the estimated duration and oxygen/battery use. These are planning estimates, not vehicle telemetry.

06 / 15
ROUTE CONTROL · EVA SIMULATION

Start and stop the route simulation

Choose Start EVA Navigation to play the simulated turn-by-turn route. The navigation banner shows the current instruction; choose Exit in that banner to stop.

Try starting and exiting the simulation. It is a visual demonstration—not live navigation or control of a rover.

07 / 15
SCIENCE CONSOLE · EXPERIMENTAL ROUTING

Request a terrain-aware science route

Choose a grid spacing, then select Find a geology-aware science route. The experimental route model combines NASA MOLA elevation, coarse MGS TES thermal-map transitions, USGS geology context, and known site hazards. It also excludes sampled cells that appear to lie inside crater-like depressions.

If a route is returned, review its statistics, confidence notes, and review flags. Download offline route briefing saves a text summary. Live NASA data may be unavailable, so try this feature when online.

Elevation data can miss shallow, small, or unresolved craters, so the route is not guaranteed crater-free. Low-confidence orbital estimates cannot detect small or changing hazards and are not certified for mission use.

08 / 15
CREW SYSTEMS · TELEMETRY

Read the modeled surface and suit gauges

The Telemetry view shows the selected site's sample temperature, pressure, and radiation values, plus estimated oxygen and battery remaining for the current route.

Open the view and inspect each readout. They are static/demo estimates—not live sensor values.

09 / 15
CREW SYSTEMS · HAZARD ADVISORIES

Inspect the site's hazard information

Open Hazards to read the selected demonstration site's terrain advisories. Select a hazard card to focus its location on the map; the Hazard zones overlay is also available in Layers.

Hazard cards describe the sample site data. A custom direct route is not automatically checked or rerouted around them.

10 / 15
REMOTE SENSING · TERRAIN & SCIENCE LAYERS

Turn map overlays on and off

In Layers, try MOLA color-shaded terrain, MGS TES thermal inertia, Global geology, Hydrous mineral detections, and Exploration zones. Each toggle adds or removes its overlay; combine layers when useful.

These orbital maps show regional context and candidate locations, not confirmed surface conditions, access, or safety.

11 / 15
REMOTE SENSING · PLACE NAMES & HAZARD OVERLAY

Find named surface features

Turn on NASA place names to show labels for the current map area. Click a label for available feature details and source links; the status bar reports place-name loading. Enable Hazard zones here to show the selected site's mapped advisory areas.

NASA Mars Trek map and place-name services require an internet connection.

12 / 15
ROVER ARCHIVE · CURIOSITY

Explore Curiosity's Gale Crater records

Try Curiosity HiRISE image and Curiosity traverse & stops. Use Go to Gale Crater to focus the map; when rover stops are loaded, open Browse named rover stops to visit a record.

These are historical NASA Mars Trek records, not a live rover position.

13 / 15
ROVER ARCHIVE · PERSEVERANCE & INSIGHT

Explore Jezero and InSight map products

Try the Perseverance site image and Perseverance traverse & site toggles. Focus Jezero with Go to Jezero Crater, then browse named locations when loaded. These overlays include the historical traverse and landing ellipse.

The InSight CTX image toggle adds another orbital surface image. Rover records are historical; imagery and landing context do not show live status.

14 / 15
REFERENCE DESK · CREDITS

Check the data and software sources

The Credits view links the Mars Trek imagery and data services, USGS references, and software used by the project. Open a source link when you want to inspect its provider information.

Source providers do not endorse this project. External links require an internet connection.

15 / 15
MISSION COMPLETE · PROJECT & CONTROLS

Know the project and make the console yours

About introduces the NeuroMars project and team. Use the sun/moon control to change the theme; select ? whenever you want to replay this field guide.

The CesiumJS browser app displays Mars Trek imagery and requests sample mission data from the local Express API; saved mission sites live in backend/Data/marsData.json.

This is a research/demo application—not an operational navigation system. NASA/USGS data can be incomplete or unavailable and cannot replace validated mission systems or expert review.
